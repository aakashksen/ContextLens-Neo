How it’s built

The pipeline, the stack and eight experiments, failures included.

  1. 1 A press freezes the conversation at one point.
  2. 2 Agents build a card from the turns before it.
  3. 3 Code drops any finding without a quote.
  4. 4 What is left is the card, or an abstention.
  5. 5 A client shows the card and the turns it cites.

Inside one press

Controller

Enforces the 8 s deadline. Returns a partial card.

Memory agent

  • search
  • memory lookup

Consistency agent

  • search
  • consistency check

Assistance agent

Writes the card. May send one specialist back.

Evidence guard

Drops findings with a missing turn, a later turn or a quote not in the turn. Nothing left means abstain.

Eight experiments

Every rule was written before its run. Every number comes from a run record.

  1. E1RetrievalHybrid won

    E1-20261103T035342386305Z, dev

    Rule, first
    Highest mean recall at 5 wins, ties go to MRR. Eight settings: dense, BM25, hybrid and hybrid with a reranker, each over one-turn chunks and 3-turn windows.
    Result
    Hybrid over one-turn chunks won. Its lead over dense and over the reranker is close to a tie, so there is no claim that reranking helps.
    What failed
    No E1 figure is stated. The record fails its own check: the QMSum manifest was not committed before the run. The held-out search benchmark in E8 stands in: MRR 0.530 and recall at 5 of 0.700 over 367 QMSum questions.
  2. E2Context shapesMemory raised answering

    E2-20261104T011721815005Z and E2-20261104T011906729928Z, 148 dev cases

    Rule, first
    Written before the run. The report does not restate it; docs/DECISIONS.md holds it.
    Result
    Recent turns, the full conversation and retrieval all abstained when they should (0.938) but answered only about two thirds of answerable cases (0.659 recent, 0.674 for the others). Adding memory raised answering to 0.947 and cut correct abstention to 0.5. A cloud arm abstained on every insufficient case (1.0) and answered 0.871.
    What failed
    A short-id citation bug dropped true findings, so E2 and E3 were rerun. A number rule that compared spelled numbers as text dropped true findings too. The cloud arm’s rerun under the fixed rule was cancelled, so its numbers understate it. A mode-defining prompt was tried, answered fewer answerable cases, let an injected claim through, and was reverted.
  3. E3Agent against teamTeam abstained less

    E3-20261104T011047113852Z, 148 dev cases

    Rule, first
    Score is the mean of groundedness and the lower of the two abstention sides, the answer rate and the abstain rate. Written before the run.
    Result
    The single agent abstained correctly on 0.875 of insufficient cases and answered 0.947. The team abstained correctly on only 0.312 and answered 0.977, at 1535 tokens per card against 634.
    What failed
    The controller marked a card answered whenever the consistency agent wrote any finding, even “the transcript does not give this detail”. Memory’s correct “not enough evidence” was outvoted. Found by reading the saved rows.
  4. E3bTeam fixesGain in abstention

    records E3T-20261104T131120203662Z, E3TS-20261104T173517541324Z

    Rule, first
    One fix allowed, then once the combination that could ship. That team becomes primary only if it beats the single agent on the new cases under both judges.
    Result
    On new template-built cases the shippable team abstained correctly more often than the single agent, with a lower grounded rate under the cloud grader. Its gain is in abstention, not in the accuracy of what it says. It became the primary system of the test.
    What failed
    Those case labels are template-built and read by an agent, not a person, so no figure is claimed. The fix’s gain on old cases is in-sample, because the idea came from reading those rows.
  5. E4ContradictionsCascade kept

    records E4-20261103T102658868337Z and E4-20261103T102930546849Z

    Rule, first
    Cascade threshold: the lowest false-alarm rate among points that keep the written catch-rate floor on every source.
    Result
    A DeBERTa-v3 checker had the best accuracy, the prompted checker far fewer false alarms, so both were kept in a cascade. On held-out data, macro-F1 on our scenarios is 0.729 (prompted 0.575, TF-IDF 0.189). Contradiction F1 on DECODE, on published labels, is 0.913 (TF-IDF 0.753).
    What failed
    The first run’s training loss was NaN in every setting, from float16 weights. Its records are void and the rerun loads float32. The E4 dev figures are not stated because the records fail their checks.
  6. E5Fine-tuningPrompting kept

    E5-20261106T222951150994Z, 148 dev cases

    Rule, first
    A winner rule written before any run, with one runtime for both arms.
    Result
    The adapter answered 0.962 of answerable cases against 0.212 for prompting. It abstained correctly on only 0.438 against 0.938, a difference of −0.5 (interval −0.75 to −0.25). It was slower: 23.9 s median against 7.2 s. Prompting stayed.
    What failed
    The base moved from a 4B to a 2B model for GPU memory, so this says nothing about a larger adapter. The prompted 2B card answers only 0.212 of answerable cases, so neither arm is a good product at that size.
  7. E6Promptsv3 selected

    E6-20261105T223904724358Z, E6-20261106T000633991380Z, 148 dev cases

    Rule, first
    Score is the mean of the grounded rate and the lower abstention side. A version qualifies only with a 95% interval above zero on a key measure and none below zero on the others. Otherwise v1 stays.
    Result
    v1 abstained 0.812 and answered 0.947. v3 abstained 0.812 and answered 0.955, at 602 tokens per card against 634, and was selected. v4 abstained on everything (1.0) and answered 0.311. v6 answered 0.977 but abstained correctly on only 0.312.
    What failed
    v3’s gain is in groundedness under Haiku. Sonnet, grading the same rows, did not confirm it as measurable. No groundedness figure is stated.
  8. E7Streaming speechFixes dropped

    records E7-20261107T003136847403Z, E7-20261107T011123157183Z, E7-20261107T023701185115Z

    Rule, first
    A latency fix is kept only if p50, p95 or the count of deadline-cut cards improves, with the answer rate on the 33 clean questions no worse.
    Result
    After a gain fix to the test audio, streamed word error rate is 0.376 and speaker error 0.599, on published labels. Button-to-card latency has a median of 5953 ms and a 95th percentile of 8524.4 ms, above the 8 s deadline. Both latency fixes were dropped: tokens per card rose from 1363 to 1676.
    What failed
    The fixed voice gate dropped most quiet speech, which made streamed error look far worse than offline Whisper. The gain that fixed it is a normalisation of rebuilt audio, computed over a whole meeting. It is not a live feature.
  9. E8The held-out testNo clear difference

    record E8-20261107T073426291017Z, 136 test cases, run once

    Result
    The shipping team shows no clear difference from the single agent on any quality measure. It is clearly worse on tokens per card and median latency. Table below.

Limits

  • No judge was reviewed by a person

    Haiku failed its written calibration against Sonnet and still judges, because local judging was ruled out. It leans lenient. Judged rates are in the records and left out here.

  • Restated conflicts are shaky

    Where a statement restates a conflict, both judges wobble. Those are the least reliable judged claims.

  • New-case labels are template-built

    An agent read them, a person did not. A template can be learned, so they guided development only.

  • The number rule has a known false keep

    A finding that cites N turns may say a spelled number up to N with no quote holding it. No run measured how often that happened.

  • The injection guard is weak

    It caught 14 of 60 orders and dropped 0 of 60 ordinary turns. Nothing here claims protection.

  • Audio is flattered

    The per-meeting gain is not a live feature. The fixed gate drops quiet microphones. Most cards on live audio hit the 8 s deadline. Rebuilt meetings have silence between utterances.

  • E5 used a 2B base

    It says nothing about a larger adapter.

  • E2’s cloud arm carries the old number rule’s bug

    The rerun was cancelled, so its numbers understate it.

  • Small samples

    E7’s comparison has 33 clean questions. One question moves its answer rate by several points.

  • The test split was run once

    After a configuration was chosen on dev. Which system ships is the person’s decision and has not been made.
